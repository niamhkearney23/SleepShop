Ground rules for everything

  • Always ask if unclear. There is no penalty for a question. There is a penalty for a mistake that could have been avoided.
  • Check everything twice. Names, dates, case numbers, party references — sloppy detail work costs the firm real time.
  • Confidentiality is not negotiable. Do not discuss client matters outside the office. Do not put client information into public tools (Google Translate, ChatGPT).
  • Deadlines are commitments. If you cannot meet one, flag it early — never on the day.

Section 2

Legal research notes

A legal research note is a written summary that answers a specific legal question for a lawyer. It is not an essay. It is a structured document a lawyer can read in 3–5 minutes to make a decision.

Before you start

Get the following from the lawyer assigning the task:

  • The exact question to research (write it down verbatim)
  • The context — what matter, what's the deadline
  • The scope — quick check vs full memo
  • The format expected
RuleIf you are given a vague question, do not start research until you've asked what specifically is being decided. A wrong question wastes hours.

Structure — the IRAC format

Standard Research Note Template To: [Lawyer's Name]
From: [Your Name]
Date: [Date]
Matter: [Client / File Reference]
Question: [The exact question asked]

ISSUE — The legal question in one or two sentences.

RULE — The relevant law: statute, case law, or procedure. Cite everything.

APPLICATION — Apply the rule to the facts. Weigh both sides.

CONCLUSION — Your best answer. If "it depends," say what on.

Sources

  • CLJ Law — primary source for Malaysian case law and statutes
  • LexisNexis — secondary source; commentary and cross-jurisdictional research
  • Firm precedent database — check first whether similar work has been done
Do not useFree legal blogs or general search engines as your primary source. Every proposition must trace back to a citable statute or reported case.

Worked example

Example

Question: Can a director of a Malaysian company be personally liable for a company debt if they signed the contract in their personal capacity?

ISSUE: Whether Mr X, a director who signed a supply contract without indicating his representative capacity, is personally liable for the RM 45,000 balance.

RULE: Section 20 of the Companies Act 2016 recognises the separate legal personality of a company. However, an individual who contracts in their own name may be personally liable — see Kelner v Baxter (1866) LR 2 CP 174.

APPLICATION: The contract is signed "Mr X" with no reference to the company. However, supplier invoices were addressed to the company — arguable both parties understood it was a company contract.

CONCLUSION: Real risk of personal liability. Recommend defence focus on the parties' course of dealing.

Common mistakes

Do
  • Answer the question that was asked
  • Cite every proposition
  • Show both sides of a close argument
  • Keep it to 2 pages unless asked otherwise
Don't
  • Copy-paste headnotes without reading the case
  • Bury the conclusion at the end
  • Use "clearly" or "obviously"
  • Send a first draft without proofreading
Self-test

Check what stuck

4 questions. Answer, then click Check.

Q1.A lawyer says "look into unfair dismissal" and walks off. What should you do?
Correct answer: Go back and ask. A vague question wastes hours. Confirm the exact question, context, scope, and deadline before starting.
Q2.Which of these is the correct order of the IRAC structure?
Correct answer: Issue, Rule, Application, Conclusion. IRAC is the standard structure for a legal research note.
Q3.What is the primary source of Malaysian case law you should use?
Correct answer: CLJ Law. It's your primary source for Malaysian case law and statutes. LexisNexis is a good secondary source.
Q4.Your research points strongly to one conclusion, but there's a decent counter-argument. What do you do?
Correct answer: Show both sides, then conclude. A lawyer needs to see the full picture to make a decision. Hiding the counter-argument is a disservice.
Section 3

Translations

Most of your translation work will be between English and Bahasa Malaysia. Translation errors in legal documents have consequences: cases have been lost on mistranslated single words.

Golden ruleLegal translation is not general translation. Your job is accuracy, not readability. If a source sentence is awkward, your translation should be equally awkward — do not "improve" it.

Before you start

  1. Confirm the direction (EN → BM or BM → EN)
  2. Confirm the purpose — filing, internal, or client-facing?
  3. Check whether a certified translator is needed. Court exhibits requiring certification cannot be done internally.
  4. Read the entire document before you begin.

The 3-pass process

  1. Literal. Translate sentence by sentence, staying close to the source.
  2. Accuracy check. Read source and translation side by side. Every word, every date, every name.
  3. Reading pass. Read the translation out loud.

What you can and cannot use

Allowed
  • Bilingual legal dictionaries
  • Firm's internal glossary
  • Previous certified translations in the firm's files
  • Bilingual colleagues to sense-check
Not allowed
  • Google Translate, DeepL, or any free translator with client content
  • ChatGPT, Claude, or any public AI with client content
  • Machine translation "cleaned up" and passed off as your work
  • Guessing a term you don't know
Why not free AI translatorsPasting client documents into free online tools breaches confidentiality — that content is stored, indexed, and used to train models. Even redacted text can leak facts.

Terminology consistency

Within a document, translate the same term the same way every time. "Perjanjian" is always "Agreement", not sometimes "Contract".

When to escalate

  • Any term where you're not 100% confident
  • Ambiguous phrasing that changes meaning depending on interpretation
  • Cultural/religious/customary references without a direct equivalent
  • Anything for court that needs certification
Self-test

Check what stuck

4 questions.

Q1.What is the golden rule of legal translation?
Correct answer: Accuracy over readability. The court needs to see what was actually said, not an improved version.
Q2.A client's witness statement in Bahasa Malaysia needs translating for internal review. Which tool can you use?
Correct answer: Bilingual legal dictionary and the firm's glossary. Client content in free online AI/translators breaches confidentiality — the content is stored and used to train models.
Q3.You're translating a contract and encounter "Perjanjian" three times. What do you do?
Correct answer: Same each time. Terminology consistency matters in legal documents — different words can imply different legal meanings.
Q4.An exhibit will be filed in court and requires certified translation. What do you do?
Correct answer: Escalate. Certified translations for court cannot be done internally. Ask your supervising lawyer to engage a certified translator.
Section 4

Drafting basics

You will draft affidavits, letters, and cause papers. Everything you draft will be reviewed — but the more polished your draft, the more you'll be trusted with substantive work.

Before you draft

  • Look at 2–3 recent examples of the same type of document from the firm's files
  • Confirm the parties, court, and suit number
  • Read any related documents

Universal rules

  • One idea per paragraph.
  • Number every paragraph for affidavits and submissions.
  • Refer to parties consistently — "the Plaintiff" throughout.
  • Dates in full — "3 October 2026", not "3/10/26".
  • Currency in full — "RM 45,000.00", not "45k".
  • Never use contractions. "Do not", not "don't".

Self-test

Check what stuck

3 questions.

Q1.How should a date appear in an affidavit?
Correct: 3 October 2026. Full dates in words, never shorthand or ambiguous formats.
Q2.How should you refer to the same party throughout a submission?
Correct: Consistently. Switching between "the Plaintiff" and the party's name creates ambiguity.
Q3.Before drafting a new affidavit, what should you do first?
Correct: Look at 2–3 recent examples. Never draft from memory. The firm's precedents establish the format and language expected.
Section 5

Court bundles

A court bundle is the physical (or digital) file of documents a lawyer takes to a hearing. Getting a bundle wrong is a fast way to embarrass the firm in open court.

Preparation checklist

Court Bundle Checklist
  • All documents in chronological order
  • Every page numbered, top-right, in sequence
  • An index at the front (document, date, page)
  • Tab dividers between documents
  • All copies legible
  • Exhibits marked correctly (P1, P2 for Plaintiff; D1, D2 for Defendant)
  • Multiple copies: judge, court file, opposing counsel, own use
  • Ready 24 hours before the hearing
RuleMissing document? Tell the lawyer as soon as you know. The day before is a problem. The morning of is a crisis.
Self-test

Check what stuck

3 questions.

Q1.How should pages be numbered in a court bundle?
Correct: Continuously. Pages run 1, 2, 3… across the whole bundle. Restarting per document breaks the index.
Q2.When should the bundle be ready?
Correct: 24 hours before. Anything found missing on the morning of is a crisis. Give yourself and the lawyer buffer.
Q3.You realise the day before the hearing that an exhibit is missing. What do you do?
Correct: Tell the lawyer immediately. A missing exhibit found the day before is a problem to solve. Hidden until the morning of, it's a crisis.
Section 6

Correspondence

You may be asked to draft emails or letters to clients, court staff, opposing counsel, or service providers. Everything you send in the firm's name reflects on the firm.

Rules for all correspondence

  • Get sign-off before sending. Show the draft to your supervising lawyer.
  • Tone: formal and professional. No slang, no emojis.
  • Short is better than long.
  • Reply-all with care.

Standard letter template

Standard Client Letter Dear Mr / Ms [Surname],

[Subject line — bold, specific]

We refer to [the matter / previous correspondence].

[Body: purpose in paragraph one, facts in two, next steps in three.]

Should you have any questions, please do not hesitate to contact us.

Yours faithfully,

THOMAS PHILIP
Advocates & Solicitors
Self-test

Check what stuck

3 questions.

Q1.Before sending a letter to a client, what should you do?
Correct: Get sign-off first. Unless a lawyer has explicitly told you otherwise, always show them the draft before sending.
Q2.How should you sign off a formal client letter?
Correct: "Yours faithfully" / "Yours sincerely". Formal correspondence uses formal sign-offs. Never "Thanks!" or "Cheers".
Q3.You're about to hit "Reply all" on an email thread. What should you check first?
Correct: Who is on the thread. Reply-all can accidentally send privileged content to the opposing side. Always check the recipient list.
Section 7

Escalation & feedback

Knowing when to escalate is a professional skill. So is knowing how to receive corrections without defensiveness.

When to escalate

  • Any task where you're unclear about the scope — before you start, not after two hours
  • Conflicting instructions from two lawyers
  • Anything you've drafted involving a novel legal issue
  • Any error you've made — flag it immediately
  • Any deadline you cannot meet — flag it early, propose a solution
RuleA mistake flagged early is a fixable problem. A mistake hidden is a professional failing.

How to receive feedback

  • Take notes when a lawyer explains a correction
  • Ask clarifying questions then, not the next time you make the same mistake
  • Keep a personal running log of feedback
  • Don't take corrections personally — the work is being improved, not you

How to ask for feedback

Once every two weeks, ask your supervising lawyer:

  • "Is there anything I've done recently that I should do differently?"
  • "Is there work I could be taking on that I'm not?"
  • "Where would you like to see me improve over the next two weeks?"
Self-test

Check what stuck

3 questions.

Q1.You realise you've made a mistake on a document that went to a lawyer yesterday. What do you do?
Correct: Tell immediately. An error flagged early is a fixable problem. Hidden, it's a professional failing.
Q2.Two lawyers give you conflicting instructions on the same file. What do you do?
Correct: Escalate. Never try to reconcile conflicting instructions on your own. Ask which takes priority.
Q3.A lawyer gives you a sharp correction. What's the best response?
Correct: Take notes, ask, log. Corrections are the fastest way to learn. Don't take them personally.
